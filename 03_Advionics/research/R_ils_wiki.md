## Facts [D-ag]
- Lead: localizer "108 to 112 MHz" for lateral guidance; glideslope "329.15 to 335 MHz" for vertical guidance.
- Glideslope section: the localizer uses **40 channels between 108.10 MHz and 111.95 MHz**; the glideslope has a corresponding set of 40 channels **between 328.6 and 335.4 MHz**. The pairs are not linear: LOC channel 1 = 108.10 paired with GS 334.70; channel 2 = 108.15 paired with 334.55. Other table pairs: 108.30↔334.10, 108.50↔329.90, 109.30↔332.00, 110.30↔335.00, 111.50↔332.90. **Inconsistency inside the article:** the lead says 329.15–335 MHz while the body says 328.6–335.4 MHz. The body plus channel table is more detailed; ICAO Annex 10 Vol I (not read) is the authority.
- The glideslope normally sits at 3° above horizontal (London City at 5.5° is a noted exception).
- Principle: the carrier is modulated by **90 Hz and 150 Hz** tones. CSB (carrier + sidebands) and SBO (sidebands only) radiate from the array. The receiver measures the **difference in depth of modulation (DDM)**, which gives an angle independent of range. On the centreline the tones cancel and the needle centres; 90 Hz dominance and 150 Hz dominance sit on opposite sides.
- An HTML-commented (hidden) line in the source says full-scale deflection equals a DDM of 15.5 %. It is **not displayed** in the article and not cited, so it is not used here.
- Categories (ICAO/EASA table): Type A DH ≥ 75 m. Type B CAT I: DH ≥ 60 m, RVR ≥ 550 m or visibility ≥ 800 m. CAT II: DH 30–60 m, RVR ≥ 300 m. CAT III: DH < 30 m or none, RVR < 300 m or none. FAA table: CAT I DH ≥ 200 ft, RVR ≥ 2400 ft (or 1800 ft with equipment); CAT II ≥ 100 ft and 1200/1000 ft; IIIa < 100 ft and ≥ 700 ft; IIIb < 50 ft and 150–700 ft; IIIc none.
- CAT I uses barometric altitude for DH; CAT II and III use the radio altimeter.
- Monitor shutdown: a CAT I localizer must shut down within 10 s of detecting a fault; CAT III in less than 2 s.
