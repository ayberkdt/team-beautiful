## Numbers and deployment facts [D-ag]

### Small "Discovery-class" concept (2000–2002)
- Sized to fit in the Pioneer Venus **small-probe aeroshell**: "1.2-meter diameter" (2001, Fig. 1 caption) / "1.3-meter diameter" (2002, p. 3). (Caveat: SP-461 gives PV small probes as 0.76–0.8 m; Landis's 1.2–1.3 m figure does not match — perhaps confused with the 1.42 m large probe. Flag it.)
- Wing **folds in two places**; **no folds** for the tail (2002); in the 2004 brief the tail boom is **hinged once** so the horizontal tail folds over the wing, wing has two hinges with left/right panels folding over the centre panel.
- Stabilizer area **9% of wing area**; essentially a flying wing; aspect ratio forced low by circular aeroshell (2002, p. 3).
- Deployment (2001, p. 2): "After heat-shield separation, parachute deployment, and stabilization of descent, the airplane is unfolded and is dropped from the parachute at an altitude of **65–70 km**", then slowly descends to its flight altitude.
- Deployment sequence slide (2026, slide 13): folded in aeroshell → **tail unfolds** → **wings unfold** → **leveling out** for flight.
- Flight altitudes analysed **65–75 km**; cloud-top wind ~**95 m/s**; solar flux 2600 W/m² exo-atmospheric, ~95% of it at 65 km; battery-only night crossing not feasible (2002, 2004).
- Inflatable-wing alternative (NASA Dryden I-2000 heritage) shown as deployment concept (2002, Fig. 4).

### RASC Venus airplane (larger, slide 16–21 of 2026 deck)
- Span **9 m**, airplane mass **103 kg** (incl. 10 kg payload).
- Folded into a **3.7 m diameter aeroshell** ("size of the Viking lander entry system", shape based on Mars Pathfinder).

### ARES Mars airplane heritage (2026, slide 3)
- 6.25 m span, AR 5.6, **101 kg** incl. margin; prototypes built/tested by Aurora; "Stow and deploy concepts demonstrated by ARES" (slide 9).

## Relevance for a rotorcraft
- Strongest numeric precedent for a folded heavier-than-air vehicle inside a Venus aeroshell: vehicle = **20%** of entry mass; parachute 10%; deployment mechanism 15%. Use as a first-cut mass fraction for a folded rotorcraft (rotor blades fold like wing panels).
- Release from parachute at 65–70 km, then dive/level-off.

## Caveats
- Conceptual studies; never flown. Mass fractions are scaled from Mars Airplane and Pioneer Venus, not designed.

## Quote
"the airplane is unfolded and is dropped from the parachute" (Landis 2001, p. 2).
