## Numbers [D-ag]
- VeGa (Vega) SP balloons: **3.5 m diameter**, **7 kg gondola module**, tracked **46 h**, travelled **11,000 km** when batteries exhausted; deployed at **54 km** (§2, §3.1, p. 1–2).
- JPL-built SP balloons for **54 km**, acid tolerant; "A balloon of **7.4 m** diameter can support a gondola module with a mass of **110 kg** including a science payload of 25–30 kg" (§3.1, p. 2).
- Google Loon (air-ballast) flew up to 190 days at up to 20 km ("equivalent to 70 km on Venus") (§3.2).
- Variable-altitude balloons: nominal range **50–60 km**; extended range 40–60 km (§3.2).
- Solar airplane: must fly at airspeeds "approaching **100 m/sec**" eastward against super-rotation to stay on the dayside; cannot traverse the night side with current storage; "requires **multiple wing folds** to package it in the aeroshell, a deployment system that ensures a smooth transition from entry to flight" (§3.3, §4.2.2).
- VAMP hybrid airship: inflated **in space before entry**; ballistic coefficient "two orders of magnitude lower than the rigid aeroshell"; rises to 60 km by day, sinks to 50 km at night; needs Venus orbit insertion (propulsion) to reduce entry speed; maturity of the inflated entry rated **low**; scale-up needs 4–5 development cycles, doubling linear scale per step (§3.4, §4.3.1).
- Reference payload 10 kg; scaling: 30 kg payload → ~**750 kg arrival mass** (SP balloon) and ~**1200 kg** (variable-altitude balloon); altitude-controlled balloons cost +30–50% arrival mass vs SP (§4.2.1).
- Sondes: as small as **5 kg** with **1 kg** payload could survive to the surface (§4.2.3).
- Entry technology: rigid aeroshell maturity "Very High"; HEEET replaces carbon phenolic, "which is no longer produced", allowing shallower entry (§4.3.1).
- Descent & deployment: the four non-VAMP concepts are deployed after descent "to an altitude at or even below where the operational phase will be conducted"; SP balloon most mature for deployment (cites Hall et al. 2017); variable-altitude balloons and **solar airplane = moderate** maturity for descent/deployment (§4.3.2).

## Relevance for a rotorcraft
- VAPS is the closest NASA precedent for a *heavier-than-air* vehicle deployed from a rigid aeroshell (the solar airplane): fold → aeroshell → parachute → unfold in air, judged "moderate" maturity. A rotorcraft would share the same deployment risk class.
- Hybrid vehicles that enter in a conventional aeroshell "were not considered" (p. 4).

## Caveats
- Summary report only; detailed solar-airplane deployment numbers (altitudes, timing) are not in this summary. Addendum on solar airplanes was to begin Fall 2018.
