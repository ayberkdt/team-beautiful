## What I read
Full text extraction (9 pp.), Sections 1–5.

## Numbers [D-ag]
- Top-of-atmosphere intensity ~**2622 W/m²**, < 100 W/m² at surface; Venera 11 (SZA 17°) and
  Venera 13 measurements used (§2).
- Optimal 2J GaInP/GaAs design: **GaInP top subcell ~200 nm thick** is optimal for all
  altitudes **10–50 km**; an AM0-optimised standard cell is not (§2, Fig. 3).
- Fabricated Venus cell (21 km / 300 °C design, GaInP 220 nm): Jsc 4.72 mA/cm², Voc 1.10 V,
  FF 0.68, P ≈ 35.3 W/m² at 21 km conditions (§3).
- Temperature effect under 1-sun AM0 (1367 W/m²): **Voc 2.27 V at 25 °C → 0.72 V at 465 °C**;
  Jsc stable ~14 mA/cm² up to 200 °C; FF 0.86 → 0.59 (§4).
  → average dVoc/dT ≈ −3.5 mV/°C for the 2J cell (my calc, (0.72−2.27)/440).
- Surface-like test (465 °C, 67 W/m²): Voc 369 mV, Jsc 0.83 mA/cm², FF 0.54, 1.6 W/m², η 2.45 %.
- Modelled surface-optimised cell: η 6.4 %, 4.3 W/m².
- Durability: some cells degraded after 4 weeks at 465 °C; best lasted 7 weeks; post-soak AM0
  efficiency 20.5 % at 25 °C (initial ~23 %); one cell fell 23 % → 17 % after 4 weeks (§5).

## Relevance (mine)
For a 50–60 km rotorcraft (≈ 0–75 °C) the high-temperature work is not the driver; the
useful facts are the spectrum-optimised thin-top-cell design and that AM0 3J/2J cells remain
current-limited by the middle/top cell in the clouds (consistent with Landis & Haag 2013).
