## Numbers [D-ag, slide no.]
- Heritage range: Mars Viking ~25 W/cm², 0.05 atm → Galileo ~30,000 W/cm², 7 atm (s.3).
- Pioneer Venus and Galileo used carbon phenolic; heritage CP "no longer viable" (s.3).
- Hyperbolic entry velocity at Venus 10.5–12.5 km/s; deceleration starts ~100 km (s.6).
- Historical entry systems (s.7): Venera 9–Vega 2 sphere ~1600 kg, β 370–412 kg/m²,
  D 2.4 m, γ −18…−23°; PV small probes 45° sphere-cone 88 kg, β 180, D 0.77 m;
  PV large probe 317 kg, β 188, D 1.42 m, γ −32.4°.
- For EFPA between skip-out and about −12°, peak deceleration < 100 g (s.8).
- **HEEET optimized rigid aeroshell (VITaL 3.5 m)** (s.11): β 225–300 kg/m²,
  EFPA −10.5…−12.5°, **peak q 1600–2100 W/cm²**, peak 55–75 g, heat load 20–30 kJ/cm².
- **ADEPT deployable** (s.12): β 25–40 kg/m², EFPA −8.5…−9.5°, **peak q 350–400 W/cm²**,
  35–45 g, heat load 7.5–10.0 kJ/cm².
- HEEET mass efficiency 40–50 % lower than CP for equivalent entry (s.10).
- Risk flag: EFPA steeper than −25°, V > 11.5 km/s and high β carry higher risk because
  ground test facilities cannot reach those conditions (s.10).
- VITaL rigid CP: entry system 1051 kg / total 2102 kg; ADEPT-VITaL (6.0 m): 807 kg /
  1621 kg; ~23 % mass reduction (s.14).

## Caveats
Slides, not peer-reviewed. Ranges are design-study envelopes, not flight data.
