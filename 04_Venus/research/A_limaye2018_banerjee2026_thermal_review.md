# A_limaye2018_banerjee2026_thermal_review — Thermal-structure reviews (variability, stability, VIRA validity)

## Citations

1. Limaye, S. S., Grassi, D., Mahieux, A., Migliorini, A., Tellmann, S., Titov, D. (2018), "Venus atmospheric
   thermal structure and radiative balance", *Space Sci. Rev.* 214:102, doi:10.1007/s11214-018-0525-2
   (open access). **Read:** full PDF downloaded (40 pp.); text of pp. 1–4, 19–23 read closely.
2. Banerjee, S., Choudhary, R. K., Tripathi, K. R., Ambili, K. M., Oschlisniok, J., Tellmann, S., Imamura, T.,

## Numbers / statements [D-ag]

From Limaye et al. 2018:
- Pole–equator temperature differences up to 65 km are ~10 K (citing Kliore 1985, Seiff et al. 1985) (p. 3).
- Longitudinal differences surface→~70 km are < 5 K at low latitudes, ~20 K at > 60° (p. 3).
- VEGA balloons at ~54 km, 5°N and 6°S: consistent 6 K difference (p. 3).
- Winds at 10 km as fast as 5 m/s (Counselman et al. 1980) (p. 3).
- Radio occultation vertical resolution ~500 m in 40–80 km; IR retrievals ~2.5 km (p. 3).
- VeRa: 1-bar level is "about 50 km"; mean lapse rate ~10 K/km below the tropopause; Venus radius 6051.8 km
  used for altitude (Fig. 15 caption, p. 20).
- 1-bar temperature has an equator–pole gradient of ~30 K (VeRa; p. 19).
- Tropopause temperatures 260–280 K at low latitudes, 220–230 K at 70°, ~240 K at poles (Fig. 16 caption).
- Static stability (Fig. 17, from Tellmann et al. 2009): extended low/near-neutral stability layer in the
  middle cloud at mid/high latitudes; **equatorial region has only shallow layers of low static stability**
  (p. 20). Akatsuki RO stability profiles 40°S–40°N shown for morning (01:10–07:10 LT) and afternoon
  (16:10–17:30 LT).
- Cloud top altitude decreases from ~67.2 km at low latitudes to 62.8 km at the poles (Lee et al. 2012) (p. 22).
- Gravity-wave activity (vertical λ ≤ 4 km): only very shallow waves in the adiabatic middle cloud, high
  amplitudes ≥ 65 km (p. 22).
- **Low Richardson number between ~45 and ~60 km at all latitudes** (Piccialli et al. 2012) → possible
  turbulence in the lower and middle cloud (p. 23).
- Small-scale negative–positive temperature excursions of 5–10 K within ~1 km in the cloud layer poleward of
  60° (Herrmann et al. 2015, VeRa open-loop) (p. 21).

From Banerjee et al. 2026:
- Data: VEX (n = 315) and Akatsuki (n = 113) RO profiles with |lat| < 50° (Fig. 2).
- Low latitudes: T decreases monotonically with altitude, no inversions; cold collar beyond ±50° above ~60 km,
  10–15 K colder than surroundings, centred ~65° (p. 5).
- VCD–RO differences below ~60 km at ±30° are "typically less than 5K"; VGRAM (VIRA) shows **larger
  differences (>5 K) in low and mid latitudes up to ~60 km, VGRAM underestimating** temperatures relative to
  RO; at high latitudes VGRAM overestimates by >10 K at 60–80 km (pp. 5–6).

## Relevance

- Use ±5 K as a minimum temperature uncertainty band at 50–60 km, low latitudes, if VIRA is the baseline;
  ±10–15 K poleward of ~50° above 60 km.
- Note apparent sign conflict: Banerjee et al. say VGRAM is *colder* than RO at low latitudes ≤60 km, while my
  comparison with the 1978 PV probes shows VGRAM *warmer* by 2.5–5 K (`A_seiff1985_vira.md`). Both are within
  ~5 K; real interannual/decadal variability is suggested by Banerjee et al.

## Caveats

- Review statements are summaries of other papers; I did not read the underlying Tellmann 2009 / Piccialli 2012.
- Banerjee 2026 is an arXiv preprint.
