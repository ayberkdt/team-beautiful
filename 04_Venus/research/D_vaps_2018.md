The full (long) report was not read.

## Findings [D-ag, §5]
- Relay: much greater data rates with power economies if relayed through an orbiter; no
  established relay network at Venus but Mars protocols/technology apply; platform position
  less predictable because of superrotation.
- **Preferred relay orbit: circular equatorial**, altitude a trade between data rate and
  tolerance for latitude excursions (§5.1).
- Data needs modest (except sub-cloud imaging) → compatible with a SmallSat relay (Venus Bridge).
- **GN&C (§5.2):** star sighting from inside the clouds "appears infeasible"; locating the Sun
  to better than **10°** may be impractical; **no permanent magnetic field** for attitude;
  **localisation better than 100 m** feasible on the Earth-facing side, but only ~**30 % of the
  time** because of radio-link atmospheric losses; latitude-changing platforms need accurate
  tracking.
- Variable-altitude balloons over the 50–60 km nominal range were the "sweet spot" platform
  (Fig. 5-1 caption).
- Fixed-altitude balloons up to 5.5 m are high-TRL (§6.1).
- VEGA comparison: communicated direct to Earth at very low rate, tracked by VLBI (§5).

## Caveats
