## Other statements [D-ag] (page in the Space Sci Rev PDF)

- Main deck ~48 to ~70 km; upper cloud 57–70 km (photochemical, UV absorber, absorbs ~half the solar energy
  the planet receives); 1–2 km gap of reduced extinction at ~56 km often separates upper and middle cloud;
  density increases with depth to a maximum at ~50 km (p. 5).
- **Optically thick layer at 47–50 km** seen by PV Large probe and Vega-1/2 LSA; not seen by some probes →
  strong variability of deep cloud (p. 4). Discrete haze layers ~46 km (and 43 km, Vega ISAV); lower haze
  down to ~30–33 km (pp. 4, 6).
- Middle/lower cloud: tri-modal radii 0.15–0.2 µm, 1–1.25 µm, 3.5–4.0 µm; mode 3 existence controversial (p. 5).
- **Temperature gradient in middle/lower cloud close to adiabatic, stability ≈ 0** (citing Seiff 1985, Limaye
  2018) → convection dominates there (p. 5).
- Tropopause ~60 km; zonal wind max 100–120 m/s in the upper cloud (p. 5).
- **H2SO4 concentration:** nearly all in-situ and remote data consistent with **75–90 wt%** H2SO4 (p. 37);
  ground-based (Arney et al. 2014) mean 79 ± 4 % (p. 31); ~84 % at low latitudes to ~90 % at high latitudes
  from VIRTIS band ratios (p. 30–31); Krasnopolsky model: **98 % at cloud bottom (~48 km) decreasing to 80 %
  in the upper cloud**, 70 % minimum at 90 km (p. 42).
- Large spatial variations of mass loading in the lower cloud 48–50 km (Ragent & Blamont 1980; Marov 1980) (p. 37).
- Below ~40 km H2SO4 vapour thermally decomposes to SO2 + H2O (p. 37).
- Mean cloud opacity at ~2.0 µm ~20 (Arney et al. 2014), 1.7–1.8× the Crisp 1986 model (p. 30).
- H2SO4 freezing: for VIRA equatorial conditions the phase change of cloud droplets occurs at **~57 km**
  (Fig. 36 discussion, p. 49); supercooled acid may become glass-like.
- Upper haze scale height 4.4 ± 1.0 km above 75 km (p. ~9).

## Rotorcraft relevance (my inference)

- In the 50–56 km operating band the vehicle is inside the **middle cloud (τ 8–10 per ~6 km)**: continuous
  exposure to ~75–98 wt% H2SO4 droplets (~300 small + 50 mode-2 + 10 mode-3 particles per cm³) →
  materials and blade-erosion/corrosion design driver; optical sensors see visibility of order a km
  (my inference from τ ~1.5 per km, not computed rigorously).
