# E_19 — Venus Flagship Mission study (2020): aerobot numbers

- **Citation:** *Venus Flagship Mission Study* (2020), NASA Planetary Mission Concept Study report; PI Martha S. Gilmore
  (Wesleyan), Deputy PI Patricia M. Beauchamp (JPL) (title page).
- **URL:** `https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/venus-flagship-mission.pdf`, accessed 2026-11-05; local
  `D:\tmp_ideathon\venus_research\pdf\vfm2020.pdf`. Read level: Sect. 3.2.3 "Aerobot" and Table 8 (pdftotext) **[D]**.

## Values [D]
- Launch 6 June 2031 (Falcon Heavy expendable); orbiter, lander, aerobot, two SmallSats (fact sheet/timeline).
- Aerobot: helium **variable-altitude balloon, 52–62 km**, minimum 60-day mission; **15 m** pumped-helium zero-pressure
  balloon with a **7.5 m** internal super-pressure chamber; pumping He into the inner chamber lowers buoyancy; venting
  raises it (fast, little energy); sealed system.
- Aeroshell 45° sphere-cone, **2.8 m**; HEEET heat shield, PICA backshell; peak heating **4,000 W/cm²** (convective,
  margined, shoulder), heat load > 53,000 J/cm²; two parachutes of 27.4 kg each (assumed identical).
- Table 8 (subsystems, excluding instruments): total CBE **1,142.9 kg**, MEV **1,403.5 kg** (aeroshell 572.8 kg CBE,
  balloon system 186.0 kg, inflation system 230.8 kg, power 78.3 kg); average power MEV **124.9 W day, 35.9 W night**.
- No attitude control and no propulsion; IMU for disturbance data; altitude from SmallSat ranging to ±1 km; 2 Gbit
  storage; one 10-minute contact per day via SmallSats; booms 0.5–2.0 m for anemometer, antennas, magnetometer.
- Planetary protection: Venus is Category II (PPIRB 2019, as cited).
