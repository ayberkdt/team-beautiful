## Values [D] (section / page in the PDF)
- Observations quoted in the Introduction: VEGA vertical winds −3.5 to +2 m/s inside the convective layer (Linkin et al.
  1986); convective cells "from several hundred meters to tens of kilometers" (Kerzhanovich et al. 1986). Earlier models:
  Baker et al. 1998 updrafts/downdrafts over 15–30 km, vertical wind 2–7 m/s rising with solar flux; another model up to
  ~15 m/s.
- Reference LES (equator, noon): convective layer 1.0×10⁵ to 3.8×10⁴ Pa (48–53 km), polygonal closed cells ~10 km wide
  (abstract); vertical wind −1.6 to +1.2 m/s at 4.7×10⁴ Pa (weaker than VEGA); broad updrafts in cell centres, narrow
  downdrafts at edges (radiative cooling drives the convection); cells ~6 km wide, cell aspect ratio ~3 (Sect. 3, p. 5).
- VeRa: equatorial convective layer ~49–59 km; observed static stability up to 25 K/km above it (model 15 K/km) (p. 5).
- Local time (Sect. 4.1): at midnight the layer is 48–53.3 km, vertical wind −2.9 to +2.2 m/s, cells ~15 km wide;
  temperature perturbations −0.35 to +0.5 K. "The VeRa profiles exhibit very low variability with local time."
- Without large-scale dynamical heating (radiative-convective forcing): layer 47.5–56.4 km, vertical wind −4.7 to
  +2.8 m/s, gravity-wave temperature perturbations ~0.6 K (Sect. 4.2).
- Gravity waves above the layer: horizontal wavelengths ~1–5 km (up to ~10 km locally at midnight), vertical
  wavelengths up to 3 km; group velocities −0.39 to −0.79 m/s (vertical), 0.18–1.8 m/s (horizontal); horizontal phase
  speed 1.43–3.1 m/s (Sect. 3.3).

## Use
Shape and size of convective cells for the simulator's gust field; supports σ_w ~0.7 m/s with strong, narrow downdrafts.
