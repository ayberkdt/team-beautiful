## 2. Synopsis and conclusion (p. 1, p. 27)

- Straight and level at **184 KIAS**, the canopy "broke up without warning" (p. 1). Pilot seriously injured, bailed out with parachute;
  aircraft hit an unoccupied block of flats.
- **AAIB cause (p. 1, p. 26, p. 27):** lack of appropriate bonding between inner and outer canopy frame at the front →
  localised stresses in the PMMA transparency → fatigue crack initiation/propagation → catastrophic failure at critical crack length.
- One safety action by the manufacturer (POH amendment, p. 27) and two Safety Recommendations (2024-004, 2024-005) to EASA.

## 4. Aircraft (p. 7)

- All-composite, fully aerobatic, two seats, certified **−10 g to +10 g**. First certified by EASA **October 2019** to **CS-23, Level 1,
  low-speed** (0–1 passengers, VNO ≤ 250 kt).
- Three-blade variable-pitch propeller. **Garmin G1000 EFIS** with data recording (footnote 1, p. 3). Flown from the **rear seat**;
  dual controls, simplified instruments in front.
- G-MIIL: built 2021, UK-registered August 2021, ~40 flying hours; CofA due to expire 14 Sep 2022.

## 5. Canopy and locking mechanism (pp. 8–11, 20) — the facts the retrofit design rests on

- Single-piece tear-drop **PMMA** transparency bonded in a **carbon-fibre prepreg** (EP121-C20-45) frame: inner U-channel + outer skin,
  bonded with **Loctite EA 9395** two-part epoxy on peel-ply surfaces. Transparency bonded into frame with urethane adhesive (p. 8).
- PMMA thickness 2.9 mm (front) to 3.5 mm (rear) (p. 8). Canopy **generates lift** in flight (p. 8).
- **Hinged on the right** by three steel hinge pins/brackets; opens to the right; restrained open by a woven nylon lanyard (p. 8).
- Small sliding window on the left side (p. 8). Rubber weather seal round the frame base (p. 8).
- **Locking: three steel shoot bolts** engage **roller lugs** on the fuselage along the **left** cockpit edge. Shoot-bolt end is a **ramp,
  15 mm long, 5 mm rise**; mechanical advantage **3:1** when the rollers travel up the ramp (compresses the seal) (p. 8).
- The hinges, shoot-bolt mechanism and guides are fitted **before** the outer skin is bonded on (p. 8) → the mechanism is enclosed.
- Two identical handle sets (front and rear cockpit). Shoot bolts on a **linkage tube held locked by a tension spring**; sliding
  lock/release handle protrudes through the frame and is squeezed towards a fixed handle to unlock (p. 9).
- **Handle centre distance: 70 mm** when open or correctly locked; **60 mm in the partially locked condition** (handle released with
  canopy closed: spring moves it only until it "just enters the green lock area") (p. 9, Fig. 5).
- To fully lock, the handle **must be pulled rearwards manually** (p. 9, Fig. 6); on another Extra NG this gave a distinct "clunk",
  felt and heard (p. 10).
- Fig. 7 (p. 10) shows shoot-bolt engagement fully vs partially locked; these parts are **"completely hidden from view"** normally.
- Forces measured on another Extra NG (p. 20): open from fully locked **136 N**; partially → fully locked **60 N**;
  open from partially locked **90 N**.
- **"There were no observable visual differences"** at the frame/fuselage edge between fully and partially locked (p. 20).
- AAIB analysis: partial lock → roller not fully along the ramp; to drive bolts open from partial lock needs **≥ 270 N**
  (3 × the 90 N) (p. 24); positive g > 1 compresses the seal and lets the spring ease the bolts to full lock (p. 25).

## 6. Procedures (POH §7.7) (pp. 10–11, 27)

- POH at the time: "Release the locking handles. Verify aft handle reaches the locked position." (p. 10)
- Emergency: normal opening; in flight low pressure flips the canopy open; jettison by pushing canopy slightly forward while opening (p. 11).
- **Safety action:** POH revision **Extra NG 1.11 20221223 (23 Dec 2022)** added a schematic and the step
  "Pull the aft locking handle fully rearward to the end stop. Verify handle is in the LOCK position (green marking)." (p. 11, p. 27)
- Manufacturer view (p. 11): in level flight aero forces put tensile load on hinges/bolts; under positive g the canopy compresses on the seal
  and the spring tends to drive the bolts to full engagement.

## 7. Service Bulletin SB NG-2-22 (p. 12, p. 27)

- Issued **December 2022**, **non-mandatory** (recommended), applies to **NG001–NG038** (includes G-MIIL NG028 and SP-HMM NG026).
- Action: inject **bonding foam** into the void between inner and outer frame at the front, from the right front hinge round to the left
  front shoot bolt.
- AAIB (p. 27): foam adhesion unknown on the existing surfaces; applied only at the front, so it does not rectify anomalies on both sides
  or at hinge brackets → SR 2024-005.
- June 2022: the same improvement was embodied on one aircraft returned to the factory, **without documentation** to the owner (p. 12).

## 8. Wreckage findings (pp. 12–20)

- All three fuselage lugs correctly attached; two of three hinge brackets found; two hinge pins found (p. 13).
- Lanyard torn from the canopy frame bolt (p. 14).
- **Shoot bolts, linkage tube and handles found held in the closed-and-locked position by the spring** (p. 14, Fig. 10). Front shoot-bolt tube
  forced away from its lug; centre bolt undamaged and locked (p. 14).
- Front frame broke into small pieces; outer skin separated from inner U-channel from front lug to front hinge (p. 16).
- Bond bead mostly on one side only; peel ply showing through; "crystalline" area (pp. 16–17). Left side over the shoot-bolt mechanism:
  narrow, inconsistent bead; ~**50 mm with no contact at all** (p. 18).
- Lab (p. 21–23): **adhesive** (interface) failure, **porosity**, no cohesive failure; epoxy **fully cured** (DSC); **fatigue initiation** at
  PMMA edges at the front, cracks propagated from the front.

## 9. Related occurrence: SP-HMM, 18 March 2022 (p. 23)

- Extra NG **SP-HMM (NG026)**, Poland: canopy **opened in flight without warning**, broke up and detached; one occupant minor facial cut;
  landed safely. Similar frame damage; shoot bolts driven closed by the spring; lugs intact.
- Polish investigation **PKBWL Serious Incident/2022/1097** (footnote 6): probable cause **vibration of the canopy frame from loss of
  stiffness → shoot bolts unlocking → canopy detached**; contributing: probable earlier frame crack, **loosened rear fitting of the
  locking system**, speed close to VNE. [D] as quoted by AAIB; the PKBWL report itself not read here (see R5).

## 10. Analysis — causes considered (pp. 23–26)

- Foreign object / bird / UAV / propeller / hail: excluded (p. 24).
- **Canopy release (unlocked) in flight:** pilot "very clear" he closed and locked; left hand permanently on throttle. Partial-lock case
  considered: needs ≥ 270 N to open, positive g re-seats the bolts → **"considered implausible"** (p. 24–25).
- **Structural failure:** bonding non-conformities vs the assembly drawing (Figs 19–20) → differential load transfer → fatigue (p. 25).
- Shoot-bolt bends show damage during in-flight break-up; spring then pulled bolts into an "apparently locked position" (p. 25–26).
- Survivability: parachute saved the pilot; a helmet with visor would "probably" have avoided the injury (p. 23–24).

## 11. Safety recommendations (p. 26–27)

- **SR 2024-004** (to EASA): ensure canopies on all Extra NG are manufactured to meet the certification standards and withstand expected
  aerodynamic and flight loads.
- **SR 2024-005** (to EASA): assess the effectiveness of SB-NG-2-22 in rectifying inadequate bonding.

## 12. Appendix A — BFU comments (State of Design and Manufacture) (p. 28) — **critical for our framing**

- The BFU **does not agree** with the fatigue-cracking scenario: it "does not explain how the locking mechanism opened".
- BFU: airflow in forward flight forces the mechanism into the locked position, not open.
- BFU conclusion: **"most probable that the canopy was not completely locked prior to the flight"** and the mechanism opened due to air
  loads and vibration; "anything but unlikely during level flight".
- BFU agrees inadvertent opening by the pilot is "quite unlikely".

## 13. What this means for the challenge [Y]

1. The official AAIB cause is **structural (bonding → fatigue)**, not an unlocked canopy. But the **State of Design (BFU) formally dissents**
   and says incomplete locking is most probable; the manufacturer changed the POH locking procedure; the Polish SP-HMM investigation found
   **shoot-bolt unlocking** (after loss of stiffness). So "unlocked canopy" is a live, contested hypothesis on this exact type — which is
   exactly why a *locked*-state alert is valuable: it would have **settled the dispute** (a recorded "locked" discrete on the G1000 data card).
2. The mechanism has a **measurable partial-lock signature**: handle centre distance 60 mm vs 70 mm (10 mm travel), with no visible
   difference at the frame. A sensor on the **aft handle / linkage tube travel** can discriminate full vs partial lock; a sensor on the
   frame edge ("closed") cannot.
3. The spring pulls the bolts back to "apparently locked" after a break-up → a post-event bolt-position reading is not proof of pre-event
   lock; a **time-stamped recording** of the lock state is.
4. A latch alert does **not** detect bonding/fatigue; that needs inspection or structural-health monitoring (separate option).
