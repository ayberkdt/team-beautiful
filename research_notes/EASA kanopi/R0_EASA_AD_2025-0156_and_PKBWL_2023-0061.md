## EASA AD 2025-0156 (3 pages, all read)

- Source PDF obtained by R1 from EASA's interim AD archive (ad.easa.europa.eu was under maintenance); see `R1_easa_ad_2025-0156.md`.
- Issued **21 July 2025**, effective **4 August 2025**; DAH Extra Aerobatic Aircraft GmbH; type **EXTRA NG**, TCDS **EASA.A.620**;
  ATA 11 placards and markings — modification; AFM amendment. Applicability: **all serial numbers** (p. 1).
- Definitions: the SB = Extra **SB-NG-1-24**; new placards P/N **EX-01106.01-(X)**; Group 1 without / Group 2 with new placards;
  s/n **NG065 and up** have them from production (p. 1).
- **Reason (p. 2):** occurrences of canopy **opened or detached during flight**; "in most cases, the root cause was a partially or
  improperly locked canopy"; could cause canopy separation, structural damage, **loss of control**, injuries on the ground.
  Extra improved the **placards** at front and rear locking handles, issued the SB and updated the POH/AFM limitations section.
- **Actions (p. 2):** (1) Group 1: within **3 months or 25 FH**, replace canopy placards per the SB; (2) before next flight after (1),
  amend AFM Limitations (POH/AFM **revision 16** or later acceptable); Note 1: (1)+(2) may be done by the **pilot-owner**
  (M.A.803 / ML.A.803); (3) a replacement canopy must carry the new placards. Ref. publication: SB-NG-1-24 Issue A, 19 Dec 2024.
- **No sensor, warning or interlock is required** by the AD (nothing in the text). AMOC possible (p. 3).

## PKBWL Final Report 2023-0061 (dated 11 June 2024; 8 pages, all read)

- Extra NG **SP-HMM**, **4 August 2023**, EPKP (Kraków-Pobiednik); occurrence class SCF-NP (p. 1).
- Course (p. 2): aerobatic flight with a passenger; at ~1 000 m AGL after an aileron roll the **canopy opened in level flight**,
  broke its limit strap and hit the right wing; emergency landing with the canopy resting on the wing; no injuries.
- Damage (p. 4): pawls "released spontaneously"; hinges held the canopy; frame and hinge-bracket bonds damaged; canopy replaced.
- p. 4: this is the **second** such event on the same aircraft in 15 months (first: 2022/1097, 18 March 2022, cause quoted:
  self-excited vibration from local loss of frame stiffness → release of the pawls; contributing: probable frame fracture, **play in
  the aft retainer of the lock hardware**, speed close to VNE).
- **Findings (p. 6):** (1) BFU and the TCH deemed the pawls were **engaged only partially**, "which technically is not precluded by the
  cockpit latching system" if the pilot does not make sure the rear slider handle is at max. aft; (2) mechanism operated properly;
  airflow alone did not unlatch it; (6) all three roller brackets and pawls intact and in "Locked" — "precludes any reasonable
  spontaneous release"; (9) **possible that the passenger inadvertently loosened the locks** during an aerobatic manoeuvre.
- **Causes (p. 6):** (1) design did not preclude partial engagement; (2) locks might not have been fully engaged before flight;
  (3) aerodynamic suction + vibration in high-speed flight could drive partially engaged locks towards open.
- Safety recommendations: **none** (p. 7).
- **TCH actions (pp. 7–8):** POH **Rev. 11** (pre-flight check §4 and §7.7 reworded; new in-cockpit "LOCK" legend, Fig. 3);
  SP-HMM mechanism modified per **ECO-NG-22-09 and ECO-NG-23-07** (new pawl contour for extra retention + an **additional spring**);
  **NG061 onwards** standard; preventive retrofit manual **AI-NG-02-23**, offered free at the factory; SB-NG-2-22 recommended.

## What this changes in our argument [Y]

1. The unlocked-canopy hazard on this type is **not hypothetical**: EASA itself (AD reason) says "in most cases" the root cause of
   in-flight openings/detachments was a partially or improperly locked canopy; PKBWL 2023-0061 concluded the same for SP-HMM.
2. All mitigations so far are **procedural/visual** (placards, POH wording) or **mechanical** (pawl contour, spring). None gives the
   pilot an independent, positive sensed confirmation — that is the gap the retrofit fills.
3. Finding (9) — an occupant can move the handle in flight — justifies the **in-flight** monitoring mode, not only the pre-take-off one.
4. G-MIIL itself stays a **structural** case per AAIB (bonding → fatigue); the BFU disagrees (AAIB p. 28). We present both, and
   state that a recorded lock discrete would have settled that disagreement.
