**Answer: YES, an FAA AD exists.** It is **AD 2026-04-12, Amendment 39-23272, Docket FAA-2025-5386, Project Identifier MCAI-2025-01227-A.** [D-ag, final rule p.12684]

## Key facts [D-ag]
- Applicability: all Extra Aerobatic Aircraft GmbH Model **EXTRA NG**, certificated in any category. Affected ADs: none. (NPRM §(b), (c), p.57169)
- **Effective date: 21 April 2026.** (final rule, DATES, p.12684)
- Based on EASA AD 2025-0156 dated 21 July 2025 as the MCAI. (Background, p.12684)
- JASC code 1100, Placards and Markings.
- Unsafe condition, §(e): the AD addresses "placards/design that did not provide adequate positive canopy locking or confirmation". (p.12685)
- §(g)(1): for aircraft without P/N **EX-01106.01-Canopy**, replace the front and rear canopy-handle placards **within 3 months or 25 hours TIS, whichever first**, per SB-NG-1-24 Issue A (19 Dec 2024), INSTRUCTIONS steps 1–2.
- §(g)(2): before the next flight, revise the POH/AFM Limitations per SB step 3. An owner/operator holding at least a private pilot certificate may do this and must record it per 14 CFR 43.9(a) and 91.417(a)(2)(v).
- §(g)(3): a later POH/AFM revision with identical info is acceptable. The background text says POH/AFM Revision 16 or later.
- §(h) installation prohibition: after the effective date, no canopy placard other than P/N EX-01106.01-Canopy may be installed.
- Costs (p.12685):
  - **30 US-registered aircraft** affected.
  - Placard replacement: 1 work-hour × $85 + $10 parts = $95 per aircraft ($2,850 fleet).
  - POH revision: 1 work-hour, $85 per aircraft ($2,550 fleet).
- Comments: "The FAA received no comments on the NPRM or on the determination of the costs." (p.12684) The rule was adopted as proposed.
- NPRM comment deadline: 26 January 2026. Signed 5 Dec 2025 by Steven W. Thompson.
- Docket items (API listing): -0001 NPRM, -0002 "EASA AD 2025-0156" supplemental, -0003 Rule, -0004 "EXTRA Aerobatic Service Bulletin SB-NG-1-24 Issue A" supplemental.

## Inferences [Y]
- The FAA wording "positive canopy locking or confirmation" is the clearest regulator statement that the hazard is a **lock-state confirmation** deficiency. That is the gap an alert system would fill.
- $95 per aircraft is a useful cost baseline: a sensor-and-annunciator retrofit will cost far more, so the proposal must justify the benefit above the placard fix.
