## Numbers (all [D-ag], read in this PDF)

### Venera 4 (1967) — printed p. 69
- Launch mass 1,106 kg; lander 383 kg; capsule designed for 300 g; "thick ablative heatshield".
- Entry velocity 11 km/s; experienced "as much as 300 g's"; parachute deployed at 210 m/s.
- Temperature data 33 °C to 262 °C for 93 min; transmissions ceased at ~28 km altitude (probe crushed); no surface data.
- Photo caption: "1-meter diameter landing capsule" (model in Moscow museum).

### Venera 5 / 6 (1969) — printed p. 83–84
- Launch mass 1,130 kg each. Lander 410 kg (V-5); 405 kg (V-6). Designed for 25 atm, 320 °C, **450 g** (vs 300 g for predecessors).
- Main and drogue parachutes "reduced in size" to shorten descent (no diameters given).
- Entry 11.17 km/s, braked to 210 m/s. V-5 data 52.5 min, lost at ~18 km at 27 atm. V-6 data 51 min, lost at ~18 km.

### Venera 7 (1970) — printed p. 93–94
- Launch mass 1,180 kg; lander 490 kg; designed for 150–180 atm, 540 °C.
- Entry interface 135 km altitude, **11.5 km/s**. Parachute deployed at 200 m/s at 60 km altitude (04:59:10 UT; separation 04:58:38 UT).
- Parachute possibly tore/collapsed; impact **17 m/s** at 05:34:10 UT (≈35 min after chute deploy). Surface signal 22 min 58 s.
- Carried DOU-1M "instrument for measuring maximal acceleration during braking" (no value quoted).

### Venera 8 (1972) — printed p. 110–111
- Launch mass 1,184 kg; lander 495 kg; designed for 105 atm, 493 °C; "upgraded parachute system".
- Entry **11.6 km/s**, braked to 250 m/s; **maximum g-force 335**; shock-layer gas >12,000 °C.
- Surface transmission 50 min 11 s; 470 ± 8 °C, 90 ± 1.5 atm.

### Venera 9 (1975) — printed p. 127–128 (most detailed descent sequence in the book)
- Launch mass 4,936 kg; bus 2,300 kg at VOI; lander 660 kg; packed in a **1,560 kg spherical entry pod of 2.4 m diameter**.
- Entry speed printed as "10.7 kilometers/hour" — obvious typo for **10.7 km/s**.
- Sequence: parachute-compartment cover jettisoned ~65 km → drogue + second chute (pulls off upper heat-shield half) → speed 250 → 150 m/s → long drag chute → 50 m/s → at **62 km three main canopies, total area 180 m²** → lower heat-shield hemisphere discarded 4 s later → ~20 min on mains → mains jettisoned → free fall on the lander's own **disc-shaped aerobrake** → impact **~7 m/s**, crushable toroidal landing ring.
- Surface: 53 min (limited by orbiter relay visibility). Camera height 90 cm.

### Venera 10 (1975) — printed p. 128–129
- Launch mass 5,033 kg; "1,560-kilogram lander" (= entry pod mass); peak load **168 g**; shock temperature 12,000 °C.
- Surface: 65 min (design 30 min); ended because orbiter relay set, not lander failure.

### Venera 11 (1978) — printed p. 151
- Launch mass 4,447.3 kg. Entry **11.2 km/s**.
- **Three progressively larger parachutes: 1.4 m², 6 m², 24 m²** (areas). Final chute jettisoned at **46 km**; free fall on circular airbrake with flaps for 52 min 58 s.
- Impact ~8 m/s. Surface transmission 95 min (relay-visibility limited).
- Caveat: landing date printed as "15 December 1978" though entry was "two days" after 23 Dec separation — date typo in source (actual 25 Dec).

### Venera 12 (1978) — printed p. 152–153
- Launch mass 4,457.9 kg; **lander (entry system) 1,645 kg; mass on surface 750 kg**.
- Entry 11.2 km/s; final chute released 46 km; free fall 52 min 11 s on airbrake flaps; surface relay 110 min.

### Venera 13 (1982) — printed p. 155–156
- Launch mass 4,397.85 kg; lander (entry system) **1,643.72 kg**.
- ~1 h descent; landing ~**7.5 m/s**; surface transmission **127 min** (planned 32 min); 462 °C, 88.7 atm.

### Venera 14 (1982) — printed p. 156–157
- Launch mass 4,394.5 kg. **Main parachute opened at 63.5 km, released at 47 km**; **750 kg** lander then fell on atmospheric drag alone.
- Surface transmission 57 min; 470 °C, 93.5 atm. Relay time shortened by flyby-bus engine underperformance (56.4 m/s shortfall).

### Venera 15/16 (1983) — printed p. 159
- Orbiters only (radar mappers, 5,250 / 5,300 kg launch mass); radar antenna replaced the lander. No entry data.

### Vega 1 / Vega 2 (1985) — printed p. 161–163
- Launch mass c. 4,840 kg. Descent module **1,500 kg**, released 2 days before entry.
- Balloon released "at 61 kilometers altitude"; balloon mass "around 20.8 kilograms" (ambiguous: whole balloon system or gondola). Helium-inflated.
- Balloon 1 lifetime **46.5 h**, ~11,600 km traversed; Vega 2 balloon also 46.5 h, slightly longer distance.
- Vega 1 lander surface 56 min (467 °C, 93.88 atm); Vega 2 lander surface 57 min (462 °C, 87.11 atm).
- Caveat: Siddiqi text says "to deploy balloons (two each)" — this conflicts with "the first ... balloon" and all other sources (one balloon per spacecraft). Treat "two each" as an error. The 61 km release altitude also differs from the usual ~54 km float altitude (release vs. float; see B_vega_balloon).

### Pioneer Venus 2 / Multiprobe (1978) — printed p. 148–149
- Total launch mass 904 kg. **Large Probe 316.5 kg, 1.5 m diameter**; small probes **76 cm** diameter, no parachutes.
- Large Probe entry velocity **11.6 km/s**; impact "32 kilometers/hour" (≈8.9 m/s).
- Small probes opened instrument doors ~70 km; **53–56 min** to surface. Day Probe survived impact, transmitted 67 min 37 s from surface.
- Bus burned up at ~120 km.

## Caveats
- Siddiqi gives no ballistic coefficients, no parachute diameters (only areas for Venera 9 and 11), no flight-path angles.
- "Lander" mass is used inconsistently: for 4V landers, 1,560–1,645 kg = complete entry system (sphere + lander); 660 kg / 750 kg = landed vehicle.
- Typos observed: Venera 9 "10.7 km/hour"; Venera 11 landing date; Vega "two balloons each".

## Quote (one, <15 words)
Venera 4: "This capsule could endure loads as high as 300 g's" (p. 69).
