# D_havoc_2016_acid — FEP vs PP vs PVC films in 75–85 % H2SO4 for 30 days (HAVOC)

## Citation
Jones, C. A., Arney, D. C., Bassett, G. Z., Clark, J. R., Hennig, A. I., & Snyder, J. C.
(2016). *High Altitude Venus Operational Concept (HAVOC): Proofs of Concept.* AIAA paper
(AIAA SPACE 2015 per NTRS — venue not checked), NASA Langley. NTRS 20160006580.
https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160006580/downloads/20160006580.pdf

## What I read
Section III "Sulfuric Acid Resistant Material Testing" (pp.7–10) via text extraction.

## Numbers [D-ag]
- Films tested: PVC, polypropylene, FEP-Teflon; acid 75 % and 85 %; immersion 1, 7, 30 days
  (18 samples); heat-sealed pocket samples.
- Metric: average transmittance 300–1000 nm (UV-VIS-NIR), plus FT-IR, mass, dimensions.
- **FEP and PP: negligible transmittance decay (within the 2 % spectrometer accuracy) over
  30 days.**
- PVC (75 %) browned within an hour, sharp transmittance drop after 1 day; PVC (85 %) leaked
  acid into sealed pocket; only PVC showed chemical change in FT-IR.
- Their chemical-resistance lookup (98 % acid): PP rated only to 49 °C, PVC to 22 °C.
- Airship operating altitude in HAVOC concept: 50 km; 30-day crewed mission.

## Caveats
Room-temperature immersion (temperature not stated in what I read); concentration 75–85 % is
lower than the ~85–98 % expected at 50–60 km.
