## Content [D-ag]
- **This is the most recent NASA rotorcraft-group paper on Venus aerial vehicles.** Its emphasis is rotary-wing and VTOL. Concepts are grouped as **above the clouds (> 60 km)**, **below the clouds (< 50 km)**, **near/on surface (< 10 km)**, and **dive-or-cycle (DC)** vehicles.
- **No concept in the paper is a rotorcraft operating inside the 50-60 km cloud layer.** The above-the-clouds class is "almost always" solar-electric flying wings deployed mid-air from the aeroshell (origami-like folding, dive and pull-out). For these the primary challenge is mid-air deployment, not aerodynamics, since Reynolds and Mach numbers "are typical of terrestrial applications".
- Mid-air deployment experience: successful high-altitude aircraft deployments have been "limited to aircraft with two to four wing folds" (ARES Mars airplane balloon drops). Spring-loaded unfolding of propeller blades may be needed.
- Below the clouds (25-50 km): an ARES-derived glider or propeller aircraft with an external MMRTG. The MMRTG (~100 W nominal) "will be unable to provide for sustained level flight" because of the reduced ΔT, so it would only support battery-recharged bursts of propulsion.
- Near surface: twin-hull pressurized tandem tilt-wing "Venus flyer" hybrid rotorcraft (Young & Aiken refs 5-6). Stopped cycloidal rotor (Husseyin, NASA Ames intern report). Mission duration of a few hours, limited by electronics "heat death".
- DC vehicles: rotary-wing decelerators (Young et al., 2nd IPPW, ref. 4); samara-seed probe "MiSSiVE" (Cooney 2013, ref. 13); hybrid lifting-body autogyro (~35° AoA during descent); "Petey" stopped-rotor cycling vehicle; falcon-stoop dive concept.
- **Table 2 (Venus column, surface values):** T 735 K, ρ 65 kg/m³, p 9.2×10⁶ Pa, a 410 m/s, R 189 J/kg K, γ 1.286, g 8.9 m/s². These are surface conditions, **not 50-60 km**. (Mars column ρ 0.0117, a 233; Titan ρ 5.4, a 194.)
- **Table 3 technology challenges:** new entry/deployment/release and dive/pull-out approaches for each vehicle class; morphing and stowage mechanisms; materials (incl. acid-cloud corrosion, noted in the text); RTG cowling and Stirling; actuators, motors and batteries for hot environments; instruments (DAVINCI VASI/VenDI heritage); AI/ML for GNC.
- **Table 4 Earth analogue tests:** pools, lakes or ocean; volcanic geysers; twin-hull airship surrogates.
- Next step stated: NIAC proposals.
