# B_venera56_tass1969 — Venera 5/6 descent capsule (contemporary Soviet releases, NASA translations)

## Citations
1. Anonymous (Soviet press, 1969). *Important step in the knowledge of universe: Soviet interplanetary stations VENERA-5 and VENERA-6*. NASA translation NASA-CR-101389, dated 8 June 1969. NTRS 19690018774. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19690018774
2. Anonymous (TASS, 1969). *Automatic station Venera-5 smoothly descended in the atmosphere of Venus. Station Venera-6 approaches its target*. NASA translation ST-PR-PS-10836 / NASA-CR-103439, 19 May 1969. NTRS 19690022998.

## What I read
Full text of (2) (3 pp.); chapter on apparatus construction and trajectories of (1) (PDF p. 6–8). Soviet primary releases in English translation (propaganda register, but the engineering numbers are first-hand announcements).

## Numbers [D-ag]
- Station mass **1,130 kg** (1).
- Descent capsule "shape close to the sphere, of about **1 meter** in diameter and **405 kg** weight"; two hermetic compartments (instrument + parachute); lower part has a damper against oscillations (1, p. 6).
- Parachute compartment holds **two parachutes: a main ("basic") and a decelerating (drogue)** one (1, p. 6).
- Designed for overloads "up to **450 units**", "a factor of one and one half higher" than Venera 4 (i.e. Venera 4 ≈ 300 g design) — because 1969 entry velocity was higher (1, p. 7).
- "The area of the main parachute was decreased by several factors" to shorten descent; canopy of heat-resistant fabric for **> 500 °C** (1, p. 7).
- Measurement range 0.5 to 25–27 atm (1, p. 6).
- Venera 5: braking began 09:01 Moscow time, 16 May 1969; velocity dropped from **11.17 km/s to 210 m/s**, then parachute system deployed; descent on parachute lasted **53 min** (2, p. 2).
- Departure: escape-burn ΔV ~3.6 km/s; flight times 131 and 127 days (1, p. 7).

## Derived [calc]
- m/A = 405 kg / (π·0.5²) = **516 kg/m²** for a 1 m, 405 kg sphere. With sphere hypersonic C_D ≈ 0.9–1.0 (textbook, not from this source) β ≈ 520–570 kg/m², the same order as the ~450 kg/m² that Venkatapathy & Smith list for Venera 3–6.

## Caveats
- No parachute areas, deploy altitudes or entry angles given.
- The "1.5× Venera 4" overload statement supports the 300 g Venera-4 design value (vs. 500 g in Girija 2023 dataset).

## Quote
"withstand overloads up to 450 units during atmosphere entry" (NASA-CR-101389, p. 7).
