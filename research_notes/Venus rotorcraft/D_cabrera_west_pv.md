## Numbers [D-ag]
- Four PV probes entered 9 Dec 1978 at 11.54 km/s, entry angles −25° to −69° (p.1).
- Shock-layer temperatures exceeded 10,000 K (p.1).
- Carbon phenolic TPS; thermocouples at 0.41 cm depth (stagnation) and 0.30 cm (flank) (p.1).
- Large probe: 45° sphere-cone, base radius 0.71 m, **nose radius 0.355 m** (p.2).
- Reconstruction initial conditions 137.78 km, 11.584 km/s, −31.829° (p.3).
- **Peak radiative (t = 18.6 s): 2275 W/cm² non-ablating → 2027 W/cm² with coupled
  nonequilibrium ablation (−11 %)** (results section).
- **Peak convective (t = 19.2 s): uncoupled (blowing-corrected) 968 W/cm², coupled
  1382 W/cm²** (results section).
- Literature summarised in the intro: Sutton 1974 found CO 4th-positive self-absorption
  cuts wall radiative flux 10–20 %; Moss et al. 1977 found ablation blockage cut
  convective flux 88 % (stagnation) and 58 % (flank) vs non-ablating (p.1) [A — secondary
  citation within this paper].
- Reconstructed thermocouple temperatures within 10 % of flight data (abstract).

## Caveats
Time base differs from Tauber 2012 (which uses 8.6 s for peak) — different reference
altitude/time origin; peak values (~2.0–2.45 kW/cm² radiative) are consistent.
