## Citation
Girija, A. P. (2023). *Planetary Entry Probe Dataset: Analysis and Rules of Thumb for Future Missions*. arXiv:2308.07005. https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07005 (dataset CSV: doi:10.13140/RG.2.2.17150.56641 on ResearchGate — not downloaded, login wall likely).

## What I read
Full text of the arXiv PDF (15 pages). The figures (Venus EFPA vs speed scatter, β vs mass) are images; I could not read individual Venera data points from them. Dataset CSV not accessed.

## Numbers [D-ag, read in this paper]
- Dataset n = 53 missions; built on the "probe blue book" (32 missions) + Venera data from Dutta et al. (their ref. 9) (p. 3).
- "Venera 4 entered the steepest, and sustained the highest deceleration load ever recorded for a probe (500g)" (p. 6); Venera 4 EFPA "-80 deg." (p. 8).
- No Venus entries after 1990 (p. 4).

## Caveats — IMPORTANT for Venera 4 peak g
- This paper says Venera 4 = 500 g at −80°. Siddiqi (B_siddiqi2018) says Venera 4 "experienced as much as 300 g's" and was designed for 300 g; Venera 5/6 were designed for 450 g. Venkatapathy & Smith give Venera 3–6 EFPA range (−62, −78). So "Venera 4 peak g" is **not settled**: 300 g (design/reported, Siddiqi) vs 500 g (compiled dataset; likely a computed value for the steep end of the corridor). Report both, cite both.
