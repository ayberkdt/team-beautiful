# C_EVAIR_Bulletin25: EVAIR Bulletin No 25, Years 2018-2022

- **Issuing body:** EUROCONTROL, EVAIR (EUROCONTROL Voluntary ATM Incident Reporting), with IATA GADM data
- **URL:** https://www.eurocontrol.int/sites/default/files/2024-05/eurocontrol-evair-bulletin-25.pdf
- **Date read:** 2026-11-04 (PDF text extracted with pdftotext)
- **Page refs:** pp. 3-4 summary; pp. 13-15 call sign similarity and confusion; pp. 18-19 air-ground communication; pp. 20-22 loss of communication.

## Content [D-ag]
- p. 4: in 2022, "air-ground communication" accounted for **37 %** of the top seven contributors to ATM occurrences in EVAIR. Air-ground communication "contributes the most often to level busts, runway incursions, ACAS RAs, and go-around" (p. 18).
- Call sign confusion (CSC):
  - Airlines reported **more than 800** CSC instances in 2018-2022, and over 20 ANSPs provided **more than 4,000** CSS/C reports (pp. 3-4, 15).
  - CSC reports were **9.2 %** of all reports excluding GPS outages (p. 13).
  - 2022: ATM not involved in **55 %** of CSC incidents. Direct ATM contribution **25 %**, indirect 17 % + 3 % (p. 14).
  - 2022 CSC contributors: flight crew **36 %**, phraseology **38 %**, pilot and ATCO readback **14 %**, hear-back omitted **9 %**, simultaneous transmission **3 %** (p. 15).
  - Example: a call sign "ending in 5 which looks like an S on the screen".
  - Consequences: mostly TCAS RA and level bust, sometimes **prolonged loss of communication (PLOC)**.
- Loss of communication (pp. 20-22):
  - **4 %** of all EVAIR ATM reports in 2018-2022, from **50** states and **94** locations.
  - It "very often" happens between two FIR boundaries.
  - ATM direct contribution **16 %**, indirect 16 %. In **68 %** of cases there was no ATM involvement ("on the airborne side").
  - Explanatory factors: radio availability problems (software, hardware) **42 %**; frequency-change problems (switching, appropriateness, congestion) **32 %**.
  - **Go-around (48 %)** is the event most often associated with loss of communication, typically on final approach when the landing clearance is not received. (The figure caption says "2016-2020 period".)
  - The approach phase carries the higher LoC risk according to pilots.

## Relevance to us [Y]
This is the strongest evidence for the challenge. Loss of communication is mostly **airborne-side** (68 %), dominated by radio/equipment problems (42 %) and frequency-change errors (32 %), and it ends in a **go-around in 48 %** of cases, i.e. during approach. A cockpit aid that (a) checks that the selected frequency matches the instructed one, (b) detects a dead radio early, and (c) prompts the RCF procedure would target the biggest slices. Call sign confusion is mostly a crew and phraseology problem, which display-based call sign highlighting could address.
