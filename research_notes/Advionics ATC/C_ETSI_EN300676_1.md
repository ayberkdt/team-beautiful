# C_ETSI_EN300676_1: ETSI EN 300 676-1 V1.5.2 (2011-03), ground-based VHF AM radio for the aeronautical mobile service, Part 1: technical characteristics and methods of measurement

- **Issuing body:** ETSI (European Telecommunications Standards Institute)
- **URL:** https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/300600_300699/30067601/01.05.02_60/en_30067601v010502p.pdf (latest version folder in the ETSI deliver directory, dated 3/1/2011)
- **Date read:** 2026-11-04 (PDF text extracted with pdftotext)
- **Scope caveat:** this covers **ground** equipment (base, mobile and hand-held for ground use). Airborne radios are covered by EUROCAE ED-23 (not read).

## Content [D-ag]
- Introduction and scope: VHF band **"118 MHz to 136,975 MHz"**, DSB AM, 8,33 kHz or 25 kHz channel spacing. It also covers 25 kHz ground stations "intended for ACARS data communication". The extended bands 108 to 117,975 MHz and 137 to 155,975 MHz are covered in a note.
- Clause 4: 8.33 kHz equipment must operate on all channels of the ICAO Annex 10 Vol V list of assignable frequencies. "The channel labelling used for 8,33 kHz channel spacing is based on a frequency-channel pairing in accordance with table 4.1 (bis) of ICAO Annex 10 Volume V". Transmitting is not allowed while the synthesizer is out of lock or during channel switching.
- Clause 4.1: minimum controls include "a facility to disable the squelch for test purposes" and an indication that a carrier is being produced.
- Clause 4.2 emission classes: **"6K80A3EJN for 25 kHz, 5K00A3EJN for 8,33 kHz"**, and **"13k0A2D for data"**.
- Clause 4.3: meets requirements within **one minute** after switch-on.
- Clause 7.2.3 Table 1, frequency tolerance (normal temperature):
  - 8,33 kHz equipment: **±1 ppm**
  - 25 kHz fixed base: ±5 ppm; 25 kHz mobile/hand-held: ±10 ppm
  - offset-carrier systems (25 kHz): 2-/3-carrier ±5 ppm; 4-carrier ±3,8 ppm; 5-carrier ±0,3 ppm
  - 8.33 kHz offset: 2-carrier ±1 ppm
  - Note: "Offset carrier frequencies will be those defined in ICAO Annex 10".
- Clause 7.3.3: carrier power within **±1,5 dB** of the rated maximum (normal conditions). No absolute watt value is set; power is set by the manufacturer's rating.
- Clause 7.4.1: modulation depth at least **85 %** (normal conditions) and 80 % (extreme). Maximum usable modulation depth "shall not exceed 95 %".
- Clause 7.4.4: audio response flat within +2 dB over **350 Hz to 2,5 kHz** (8.33 kHz) and **300 Hz to 3,4 kHz** (25 kHz).
- Clause 7.9.3: transmitter attack time **< 20 ms**, release time **< 10 ms** (< 5 ms in data mode). Keying time to 90 % power **< 50 ms**.
- Clause 8.1.3: receiver sensitivity (12 dB SINAD, psophometric filter) "shall not exceed **-101 dBm**" under normal conditions and -95 dBm under extreme conditions.
- Clause 8.11: squelch "mute[s] the receiver audio output signal when the level of the signal at the receiver input is less than a given value".

## Relevance to us [Y]
This answers "how does the voice signal reach the display" at the physical layer. Voice is analogue **AM** (A3E) in a 25 or 8.33 kHz channel, with an audio band of about 300 to 3400 Hz (25 kHz) or 350 to 2500 Hz (8.33 kHz). That is narrower than telephone audio and limits speech-recognition accuracy. Squelch mutes weak signals. Offset-carrier (climax) systems use 2 to 5 ground transmitters on slightly shifted carriers for wide coverage. Because AM keeps the strongest signal, two overlapping transmissions do not show up clearly (see C_SKYbrary_USiT). Any speech-to-text "translate signal" box would take its input after the receiver audio stage and should be designed for this bandwidth and noise.
