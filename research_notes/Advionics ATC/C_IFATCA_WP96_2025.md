# C_IFATCA_WP96_2025: "Communication Failure Policy Review", IFATCA'25 Working Paper No 96

- **Issuing body:** IFATCA (International Federation of Air Traffic Controllers' Associations), presented by TOC. 64th Annual Conference, 28 April to 02 May 2025, Abu Dhabi. Agenda item B.5.4.
- **URL:** https://ifatca.org/wp-content/uploads/WP-2025-96.pdf
- **Date read:** 2026-11-04 (PDF text extracted with pdftotext)
- **Use:** it quotes ICAO Annex 2 (citation "ICAO, 2024, p. 3-12 / 3-13") and PANS-ATM (citation "ICAO, 2016b, p. 15-9"). These are secondary quotations of ICAO text. The WP gives page numbers, not paragraph numbers, so I give no Annex 2 paragraph number from this source.

## Content [D-ag]
- §2.4 quotes Annex 2: a controlled flight shall maintain a "continuous air-ground voice communication watch" (p. 3-12).
- §2.5: CPDLC and satellite voice do not cancel the Annex 2 voice-watch standard. An aircraft on CPDLC still monitors HF/VHF.
- §2.6: RCF procedures have existed since the **first edition of Annex 2 in 1948**.
- §2.7 quotes the current Annex 2 (p. 3-13). In airspace where radar is **not** used, maintain the last assigned speed and level "for a period of 20 minutes" after failing to report over a compulsory reporting point, then follow the filed plan. Then proceed to the fix, hold, descend at EAT, approach, and "land, if possible, within 30 minutes" after ETA/EAT. (The radar-environment sub-paragraph, which carries the ICAO 7 minutes, is cut by "..." in the WP.)
- §2.8 quotes PANS-ATM: "separation shall be maintained between the aircraft having the communication failure and other aircraft, based on the assumption that the aircraft will comply" (p. 15-9).
- §2.10: if the RCF aircraft deviates to stay in VMC, ATC may be unable to keep the PANS-ATM separation, because ATC no longer knows its intent.
- §2.20: codes 7601, 7602, 7603 and 7604 have been discussed for pre-defined manoeuvres. Problems: these codes are routinely assigned to normal flights, and they are not treated as emergency codes by equipment. There is no global agreement, but "7601 has been implemented in Europe".
- §2.21: the ICAO Air Navigation Commission is considering options to return to the departure aerodrome, or divert to an en-route alternate filed in the flight plan.

## Relevance to us [Y]
This is the controllers' view. The whole RCF scheme depends on **predictability**: ATC separates other traffic from the trajectory it expects. An automated system that "helps" by flying a creative diversion would break that assumption. Value is added only by making the **standard** behaviour and its intent visible (timer, code, next fix). The ICAO work on more options (return to departure aerodrome, filed alternate) shows the rule set is still changing.
