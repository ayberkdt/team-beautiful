# C_FAA_FR_2012_TSO_C122a: Federal Register notice of intent to cancel TSO-C122a (anti-blocking devices)

- **Issuing body:** US FAA, Federal Register Vol. 77, No. 121, p. 37734, Friday June 22, 2012. Document number 2012-15238.
- **URL:** https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2012-06-22/html/2012-15238.htm
- **Date read:** 2026-11-04

## Content [D-ag]
- TSO-C122a covers devices that prevent blocked channels in two-way radio communications "due to Simultaneous Transmissions". The background mentions a **1984** petition asking for anti-blocking and stuck-microphone protection circuitry.
- Reason for cancellation: only **one** TSOA application since **1994**, the expected obsolescence of the equipment, and no industry interest. TSO-C128 (FAA's response to the 1984 petition, covering "unintentional transmissions") is described as effective and popular with VHF radio manufacturers.
- Existing authorisations are unaffected. Comments were due July 23, 2012. Signed June 18, 2012.
- I did not verify whether the cancellation was later finalised.

## Relevance to us [Y]
Hardware anti-blocking devices exist as a certified product class but saw almost no market uptake. The stuck-mic limiter (TSO-C128 type) is the accepted airborne remedy. A new "stuck mic / blocked call" feature needs a better business case than the 1990s devices had. Prior art exists (US patents 4932071, 4335468 appear in search results **[A]**).
