## Data
An unnumbered "sample set" of ASRS reports. The number of reports is not stated.

## Findings (qualitative; the article gives no percentages) [D-ag]
- Four main patterns behind readback errors: similar aircraft callsigns, only one pilot monitoring ATC, slips of mind and tongue, and mind-set/expectancy.
- Incident types: traffic conflicts, altitude busts, missed crossing restrictions, heading/track deviations, runway transgressions, takeoff-clearance and parallel-runway (L/R) mix-ups. The article mentions "Two reports of controlled flight toward terrain."
- Number confusion named: "one zero" vs "one one thousand", and L/R on parallel runways.
- Similar callsigns at hubs (e.g. flights 401/402/403).
- Short quote: "controller silence is not confirmation of a readback's correctness".

## Relevance to us [Y]
- "Only one pilot monitoring ATC" is a cockpit-side failure mode that a persistent visual ATC log directly addresses, because the other pilot can read it after the fact.
- Expectancy errors call for the display to highlight values that differ from the expected or previous clearance. This is a novelty cue, not just a transcript.
