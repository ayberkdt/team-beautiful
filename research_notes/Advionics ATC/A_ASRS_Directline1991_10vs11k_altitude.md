# A_ASRS_Directline1991_10vs11k_altitude — "One Zero Ways to Bust an Altitude... Or Was That Eleven Ways"

## Citation
George, D. (1991). "One Zero Ways to Bust an Altitude... Or Was That Eleven Ways." ASRS Directline, Issue No. 2, October 1991. NASA Aviation Safety Reporting System.
URL: https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/publications/directline/dl2_10k.htm

Date read: 2026-11-04. Read: the HTML page was fetched through WebFetch, which returns a model summary rather than the raw text, so the numbers below come from that summary of the page. [D-ag, via fetch summary]

## Data
191 ASRS full-form reports, 1987–1990, of altitude deviations where a misinterpreted clearance contributed. Inclusion: overshoot or undershoot of assigned altitude by 1000 ft (2000 ft above FL290).

## Numbers
- The 10 000 / 11 000 ft pair ("one zero" vs "one one") makes up 38% of the sample, more than any other altitude pair.
- The next largest category is less than 5% of the deviations.
- No counts are given for to/two, for/four, FL vs ft, or heading vs altitude confusion (checked: not in the page summary).

## Failure mode
Acoustic and phonetic similarity of "one zero" and "one one" thousand over the radio, plus expectation.

## Relevance to us [Y]
- One digit pair causes 38% of misinterpreted-altitude deviations. The display must show cleared altitude as a large number with unit. Where ASR confidence on the 2nd digit is low, show both candidates ("10 000 / 11 000?") and prompt verification rather than pick one.
- Example of a plausibility check: compare against the expected altitude from the STAR or procedure, or the last cleared altitude, to resolve.
