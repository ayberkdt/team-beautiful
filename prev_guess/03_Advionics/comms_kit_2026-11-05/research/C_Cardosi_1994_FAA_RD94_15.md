## Content [D-ag]
- Sample: **49 hours** of tower (local) voice tapes from **10** ATCTs. **11,234** controller-to-pilot transmissions, of which **8,444** were "messages of substance" and 2,790 were requests for information, salutations etc.
- "Fewer than one percent of the messages resulted in communications errors."
- **19** readback errors were found. In **7** of them the controller did not notice the error, which is "37% of the readback errors and less than one-tenth of one percent" of all messages.
- Most common readback error: confusing left and right runways with the same number, **21 %** of the 19. A further **32 %** related to taxi instructions.
- **81** instances (**.9 %** of messages) where the pilot answered with a different call sign. Fewer than half of these were corrected.
- **78** requests for repeat (< 1 %).
- Miscommunication rate below 1 % for messages with one to five elements. Messages with six and seven elements: **1.4 %** and **1.3 %**.
- The only factor associated with errors was "similar call signs on the same frequency".
- Earlier work cited in the introduction:
  - Morrow, Lee & Rodvold (1993), TRACON: readback error rate < 1 %, half "repaired" by controllers; partial or missing readbacks **3-13 %**.
  - Cardosi (1993), en route: **1-3 %** miscommunication for clearances with one to four elements versus **8 %** for five or more. Clearances with five or more elements were 4 % of messages but **26 %** of readback errors.

## Relevance to us [Y]
These are hard baseline numbers. Voice readback errors are rare (< 1 %), but controllers miss about a third of them (7 of 19), and error rates rise with message length. A cockpit "translate and display" function adds value mainly as an independent **hearback check** on long, multi-element clearances and similar call signs. A system that is itself wrong more than about 1 % of the time would be **worse** than the human baseline, which is a clear accuracy target for the pitch.
