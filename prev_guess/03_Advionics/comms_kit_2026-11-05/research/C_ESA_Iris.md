# C_ESA_Iris: "Iris for aviation"

- **Issuing body:** European Space Agency (ESA)
- **URL:** https://www.esa.int/Applications/Connectivity_and_Secure_Communications/Iris_for_aviation
- **Page date:** not shown. Related articles are dated up to 21/06/2022, so the page is probably from about mid-2022 (my inference).
- **Date read:** 2026-11-04

## Content [D-ag]
- Iris is a "high bandwidth satellite datalink", developed by ESA "in partnership with satellite communications provider Inmarsat" and coordinated with SESAR.
- First trial flights in **2018**. It was due to become operational on commercial flights across Europe from the **start of 2024**.
- **2028:** the datalink is expected to become "the primary means of communication" between pilots and controllers. This is an ESA ambition, not a regulation.
- The fetched page does **not** mention RCP 130 / RSP 160 or ATS B2. A search snippet from another ESA page says "fully operational in the European airspace since 2024 with more than 17,000 flights" and "RCP 130 and RSP 160 for ATS B2" **[A]**.

## Relevance to us [Y]
This is a forward-looking claim the jury may know. Even ESA's own vision puts data-link-first ATC communication **after 2028**. In 2026 the approach phase is voice-based, which is the gap the team's concept addresses.
