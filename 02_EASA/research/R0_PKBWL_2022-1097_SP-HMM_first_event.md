## Facts

- Serious incident, 18 March 2022, Kraków-Pobiednik (EPKP); Extra NG (TC EASA.A.620), PIC in the rear seat, passenger in
  front (pp. 2–4).
- **Precursor (p. 4–5):** on the second flight of the day the passenger reported "unusual sounds and light vibrations". The
  crew suspected the engine, ran it up and inspected the aircraft, found nothing and concluded the vibration was an illusion.
- Third flight: after accelerating to about **350 km/h** in level flight, the canopy suddenly opened, broke, and part of it
  detached; the PIC slowed to about 200 km/h and landed; minor face cut from the headset (p. 5).
- **"The aircraft was not equipped with any recording devices"** (p. 5).
- Mechanism (p. 6): three hinges on the right; sliding handle on the left connected to pawls on a common pushrod (text says
  "two pawls" but Fig. 7 labels Pawl 1 and Pawls 2 and 3, i.e. three, consistent with the AAIB); notch (dead point) at the
  locked position; front and rear handles on the same pushrod; in flight, on unlocking (jettison) the canopy moves back on the
  hinge guides and detaches.
- Fittings (p. 6–7): each fitting has two screws; the **rear fitting's screws were loose**, leaving clearance even with the pawl
  engaged, which could cause canopy vibration. Each fitting has a roller to cut friction and raise the frame-to-fuselage
  pressing force (with the rubber seal against vibration).
- **The pawls were found in "locked" positions after detachment**; "It does not mean that the canopy was detached when the
  pawls were locked"; the spring displaced the pushrod back to locked (p. 7). Same post-event artefact as G-MIIL (AAIB p. 26).
- Analysis (pp. 7–9): PIC's statement that he locked the canopy is "considered credible" because an unlocked canopy would have
  fallen off during run-up, taxi or take-off. Most probable cause: vibration from loss of frame rigidity after cracking near the
  middle pawl, after the rear fitting loosened. Groove worn by the rear slider handle shows high vibration intensity.
  Possible assembly failures on a new aircraft.

## Use in the work [Y]

1. In both SP-HMM 2022 and G-MIIL the bolts were found "locked" after the event, and neither aircraft recorded the lock state.
   A time-stamped lock discrete would remove that ambiguity (option O2 / requirement R-12).
2. A loose fitting and a vibration precursor were present one flight earlier. A bolt-depth sensor with an **analog output**
   (option O1) could trend bolt play and vibration amplitude in each lug. An amber "CANOPY CHECK" maintenance message could
   then appear before an opening. Feasibility medium; threshold setting needs flight data.
3. The PKBWL argument that an unlocked canopy "would have fallen off during taxi" contrasts with AAIB p. 24–25, which says
   positive g re-seats a partially locked bolt. The two investigations reason differently about partial-lock behaviour.
   That is a good mentor question.

## Conclusions (pp. 9–10), read in the second round [D]

- **§15 Cause:** "vibration of the canopy frame caused by the loss of its stiffness that led to pawls unlocking and detaching
  the canopy from the aircraft".
- **§16 Contributing:** (1) probable crack of the canopy frame in the flight before the occurrence (or earlier); (2) loosened
  rear fitting of the canopy locking system; (3) accelerating the aircraft to a high speed, close to VNE.
- **§17:** SCAAI proposed no safety recommendations. §18: no systemic changes proposed.
