## 2. Notes per type (only what the table cannot hold)

### CAP 232 (S1) [D-ag]
- Locking system (p. 3): "a linkage fastened to the canopy frame allowing the pilot to action a sliding rod by means of a
  handle, with a return spring holding the handle in the locked position", plus "two tubes fastened to the interior of the
  fuselage corresponding to two possible housings".
- The canopy "can be locked in a slightly-open position or completely closed" (p. 3).
- Pilots left it locked in the vent position while taxiing in summer, then closed it before take-off (p. 5).
- **Design meaning [Y]:** at the handle, both states are "locked". Only the receiver (lower tube) tells closed from vent.

### Pitts S-2B (S2) [D-ag]
- The horn sounded "if the canopy locking lever was not in the fully locked position" (p. 36).
- After the event, the locking mechanism "remained in the locked position". The pilot concluded it "could not have engaged
  correctly on the aircraft fuselage" (p. 36).
- **Design meaning [Y]:** a lever-position alarm existed and was silent. The fault was at the receiver.

### Slingsby T67 (S4, S5) [D-ag]
- Normal closing (S5): handle left open, the bolts cam the spring-loaded hooks aft and the hooks spring back to "ready".
  "In this position, the canopy is closed but not locked." Pushing the handle fully forward pulls the hooks down; an
  over-centre holds them.
- S4 p. 70: if a side load acts while closing, the hooks can "pass down the side of the channels, thus missing the
  dowels". Scratch marks showed this had happened. But the handle force was then high, which warns the pilot.
- S4 p. 70: a guide tongue missing its slot distorts the frame. The over-centre then "seemed less positive", so less force
  unlocks it. [Y: an example of distortion weakening a correctly placed latch.]
- S4 p. 71: on one aircraft the dowels could be "held by the toes of the hooks". Hooks could be held back by fouling on the
  coaming cover.
- S5: Mod M810 placard shows correct and incorrect latch positions. AAIB reproduced the incorrect modes and found one more
  (hook tip abutting the bolt). **SR 97-44**: add "appropriate markings, on and around the canopy latches".
- S5: the pilot "had developed the habit of releasing the canopy and then relocking the latches". In the climb he did
  this by reflex, and the canopy flew open.

### Aero L-39C (S6) [D-ag]
- p. 17: hinges disengage when closed, "when closed, the canopy is not attached to the aircraft unless it is also locked".
- p. 18: the external handle "can be stowed ... regardless of whether or not the canopy has been locked".
  "Visual confirmation that the handle is stowed does not guarantee that the canopy is locked."
- p. 16–17: the light stayed on. The pilot believed the switch was mis-rigged; on a previous flight he had put the light
  out by "twisting and pushing the internal front canopy lock vigorously". [Y: a sensor that is believed faulty is
  ignored; ties to cry-wolf in R4/R6.]

### ASK 21 (S12, S13, S15, S16)
- S12 p. 7.8 [D-ag]: "a safety system has been incorporated which only allows the front canopy locking handles to be
  pushed home if the rear canopy is properly locked first." Half-way front handles = rear not locked.
- S13 [D-ag]: the interlock lever ("canopy lock protection lever", left and right) moved from plastic to aluminium, "more
  durable". Optional. [Y: implies the plastic lever wore or broke in service; a BGA 2008 note on interlock wear was seen in
  a search snippet only, not read.]
- S16 [D-ag, club document]: "If the rear canopy is opened after the front canopy is closed, the inter-lock is bypassed."
- **Design meaning [Y]:** a mechanical interlock is a form of indicator (the front handle cannot reach home). It also has
  a bypass path and a wear path. It is a latch-function change, so it is outside our scope; we can only learn from it.

### Duo Discus / Nimbus-4D (S17, S18, S19) [D-ag]
- S17 p. 1: "the fuselage could be temporarily deformed due to forces related to acceleration. That deformation could
  allow the locking mechanism to move into the open position."
- S17 p. 2: another case where the crew may not have noticed "the handle in locked position but not connected with the
  fuselage pins".
- S18 (Comments): a commenter says a pilot can "close the canopy, slide the levers forward, and not have the canopy
  properly latched". Another says front-seat pilots rest a hand on the latch. FAA kept the AD.
- S18 (Related service information): the TN adds a compression spring, spring washers at the levers, and a magnet on the
  front lever with a red mark inside the left frame. [Y: the red mark is a position reference for the lever. The magnet's
  role is not stated in S18; it may hold the lever. Not verified.]

### DG-1000S (S20, S21) [D-ag]
- S21: "the rear locking rod of the rear canopy rotated out of the threads of the operating mechanism"; due to similar
  design the front is also affected. Risk: blocking the emergency release. EASA asks for a daily check that the rod end
  does not protrude over the frame contour; the FAA did not adopt the daily check.
- **Design meaning [Y]:** latch geometry can drift between maintenance checks. A sensor set at installation can drift
  out of step with the latch (same lesson as RV-14 in R7).

### Diamond DA20-C1 (S23) [D-ag]
- p. 7-11: "To close: Move both LH and RH locking handles forward." "If the canopy locking warning light is illuminated,
  the canopy is not locked properly." The light needs the Master Switch ON.
- External handles on some aircraft "do not affect operation of the inside locking handles".
- How the light is wired (one switch per side, series, or one switch) is **not** in the AFM.

### Van's RV-12 (S10) [D-ag]
- p. 5: "Once closed, the handle was turned 90° so that a tang on the end of the handle extended under the adjacent
  fuselage roll bar frame, where the tang was retained under a latch block."
- p. 7: only the handle of a replacement kit was fitted, with extra washers; the new tube was ¼ in longer.
- p. 8: Van's SL (Nov 2016): an unlatched tip-up canopy will "most likely pitch nose down abruptly".

### SportCruiser (S8, S9) [D-ag]
- S9 p. 2: "Without being latched, the hinged canopy would rest in the fully closed position. After takeoff, the
  unlatched canopy lifted from the frame rail."
- S8: the pilot reached to close a canopy that had lifted 1–2 in; it "suddenly opened almost fully".

### Europa (S11) [D-ag]
- p. 2: door flex while pulling the door down can misalign the rear bolt with its guide. A misaligned left door "can be
  difficult to identify from the left seat". A reach-over check of the other door is not always possible.
- p. 2: SM15833 adds an aerodynamic stop on the outer skin aft of the rear guide. A baulked bolt stops the lever short of
  the "indicated locked position".

### Glider clubs (S24) [D-ag, club note]
- "Different gliders have different canopy locking mechanisms." Some types (e.g. Vega) "may require a helper to check the
  canopy is locked". Check "reasonable stiffness in the locking action".

## 3. Cross-cutting failure modes (what makes "looks locked" differ from "is locked")

Hand-written.

| # | Failure mode | Where the truth is lost | Examples (source) |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Engaging element passes **beside** its receiver | At the receiver; handle and linkage reach "locked" | T67 hooks down the channel side (S4 p. 70); Europa rear bolt over the skin (S11); BD-10 pin below the lug [Y R7]; Pitts horn silent (S2) |
| F2 | **Partial** engagement (wedge, toe or tip only) | At the receiver; handle often short of the stop | Extra NG wedge on roller [Y R1]; T67 hook toes, tip abutting (S4, S5); Sling 4 short of over-centre (S7) |
| F3 | **Handle stowed / in "locked" position without the lock made** | Between handle and latch | L-39 external handle (S6 p. 18); Duo Discus "handle in locked position but not connected with the fuselage pins" (S17 p. 2) |
| F4 | A **legitimate second locked state** (vent position) | At the receiver; the handle is truly "locked" | CAP 232 (S1 p. 3); DA40 partially-open taxi setting lights the shared light [Y R0] |
| F5 | **Structural deformation** under load unlocks a correct lock | Load path; over-centre margin | Duo Discus aerotow (S17 p. 1); T67 tongue out of slot (S4 p. 70); Extra NG frame vibration [Y R0] |
| F6 | **Handle knocked** in flight by an occupant | Handle | Sling 4 passenger lever (S7); Duo Discus hand on latch (S18); Extra NG passenger [Y R1] |
| F7 | **Rigging drift / non-standard parts** | Linkage length, sensor set point | DG locking rod unscrews (S21); RV-12 mixed parts (S10 p. 7); RV-14 switch set short [Y R7] |
| F8 | **Closed-but-unlatched canopy sits flush** on the ground | Visual check of the frame | SportCruiser (S9 p. 2) |
| F9 | Canopy opens **fully when the pilot tries to re-latch or hold it in flight** | Human response after the alert | CAP 232 (S1 p. 7); T67A (S5); SportCruiser (S8; S9) |
| F10 | Second canopy or door hidden from the pilot | Pilot's view | Europa right door (S11 p. 2); L-39 rear canopy from front seat (S6 p. 17); ASK 21 interlock bypass (S16) |

## 4. Taxonomy of latch architectures

Hand-written. Families are R8's grouping [Y]; the member facts carry the tags given above.

| Arch. | Description | Members (this file) | Typical look-alike state | Common failure modes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Linear shoot bolts on one common rod**, several points, one handle | Extra NG (3 bolts, 2 sliders), Europa door (2 bolts), RV-10 door pins [Y R7] | Handle near the stop with a bolt on its wedge, or one bolt outside its guide | F1, F2, F5, F6 |
| A2 | **Single sliding rod into one of several fixed housings** | CAP 232 | Locked in the vent housing | F4, F9 |
| A3 | **Rotary hooks on pins/dowels with over-centre cam**, one handle | T67 (2 hooks), L-39 (2 pairs per canopy), Sling 4 (2 latches per door, push-pull rod) | Hooks "ready" but not pulled down; toe engagement; lever short of over-centre; external handle stowed | F1, F2, F3, F5, F6 |
| A4 | **Independent handle per side**, each locking its side | DA20-C1 (LH + RH), ASK 21 (2 levers per canopy), Grob 103 (2 jettison handles) | One side locked, one not | F2 on one side; F10 |
| A5 | **Lever on frame driving locking rods/pins into fuselage pins** (glider style, "lever forward = locked") | Duo Discus, Nimbus-4D, DG-1000 | Lever forward but pins not engaged | F3, F5, F6, F7 |
| A6 | **Single turn-handle with tang under a block** | RV-12 | Handle turned but tang not fully under the block [Y] | F2, F7 |
| A7 | **Mechanical interlock between two canopies** (add-on to A4/A5) | ASK 21 | Bypass if the rear is opened after the front is closed (club doc) | F10; interlock wear (TN 36) |

Hinge type changes the consequence, not the latch logic [Y]:
- **Forward-hinged tip-up** (RV-12, ASK 21 front): opening gives an abrupt pitch-down (S10 p. 8).
- **Side-hinged with jettison or disengaging hinges** (L-39, Extra NG, CAP 232): a closed but unlocked canopy may not be
  attached at all (S6 p. 17). Loss of the canopy is likely.
- **Aft-hinged / rearward-opening on struts** (T67 pre-mod, DA20): the canopy acts as an airbrake (S4 p. 69).
- **Gull-wing doors** (Sling 4, Europa): the far-side door is hard to check from the pilot seat (S11 p. 2).

## 5. Design implications [Y]

All of this section is R8 interpretation, built on the tagged facts above.

### 5.1 Where to sense, per architecture

| Arch. | Best place to sense "locked" | Why (failure modes it catches) | What a handle-only or linkage-only sensor misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 shoot bolts | **At each receiver**: bolt tip present at full depth in the lug (one sensor per bolt). Add **linkage full-travel** as a second, cheap channel | Catches F1, F2 per point. Linkage channel catches "handle short of the stop" for all bolts at once | F1 (bolt beside the lug: linkage is at full travel); F5 partly |
| A2 single rod, 2 housings | **At the "closed" housing only** (rod tip present in the lower tube); plus a canopy-seated sensor | Separates locked-closed from locked-vent (F4) | Everything: in the vent state the handle is truly "locked" |
| A3 hooks + over-centre | **At each hook**: hook fully down on the pin (hook rotation at the pin, or pin-in-throat). Linkage over-centre position as second channel | F1, F2, F3 | F1, F3 (L-39 handle stowed without lock) |
| A4 per-side handles | **One sensor per side**, at the receiver or at each side's latch; never one switch for both | One side not locked | The unsensed side |
| A5 lever + pins (glider) | **At the fuselage pin**: pin engaged in the canopy fitting. Lever position alone is not enough | F3 ("handle in locked position but not connected") | F3, F5 |
| A6 turn-handle tang | Tang fully under the block (sensor in the block) | F2 | Partial tang overlap |
| A7 interlock | Sense each canopy separately; do not trust the interlock as the indicator | F10 bypass | Bypass state |

Common rules for all architectures:
1. **Sense engagement at the receiver, not travel at the drive.** The S2 Pitts horn, the L-39 SAFO and BD-10 cases [Y R7],
   and the Duo Discus AD all show a correct-looking handle with an open latch.
2. **Add a "canopy seated" channel** (frame against sill). It separates F4 and F8 (closed-looking) from "closed". It also
   lets the logic show OPEN (white) on the ground and UNLOCKED (amber/red) only when it matters, which avoids the DA40
   cry-wolf case [Y R0].
3. **Put sensors on the fuselage side** where the receivers are, with targets on the canopy elements. Then no wire crosses
   the canopy break or the jettison interface. A steel bolt or hook can be the target of an inductive sensor, so no magnet
   needs to be added to the latch.
4. **Per-point indication, not a series chain** (consistent with R7 point 6).
5. **Set-point check at installation and at each inspection**, with a full-engagement gauge and a partial-engagement gauge
   (F7: DG rod drift, RV-14 set point [Y R7]).
6. **The in-flight procedure matters as much as the light.** F9: three types (CAP 232, T67A, SportCruiser) lost or fully
   opened the canopy when the pilot tried to re-latch or hold it in flight. The alert must fire **before take-off**
   (power-up / line-up phase). In flight, the message and the checklist should say "fly the aircraft, do not re-latch".
   This matches the SGC club advice (S24) and the CAP 232 pilot's own view (S1 p. 6).

### 5.2 Where a mechanical indicator can go without changing the latch function

| Indicator | Where | Precedent | Changes latch function? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Colour band / witness mark on the engaging element**, visible only when fully engaged | On the bolt, hook or pin, read through the receiver gap or a small sight window in the sill fairing | T67 red hooks visible from both seats (S4 p. 70); SR 97-44 markings "on and around the canopy latches" (S5) | No (paint or a clamp-on collar) |
| **Placard showing correct vs incorrect positions** | Next to the latch, in the pilot's view | T67 Mod M810, CAA-mandatory (S5) | No |
| **Reference mark for handle or lever "fully home"** | On the frame next to the handle | Duo Discus red mark inside left frame (S18); Extra NG "max aft" legend [Y R1] | No. But it only shows the handle, so it does not catch F1 or F3 |
| **Flag or pop-up pin driven by the bolt tip** at the receiver | Fuselage-side receiver | none found for these types | Must not add load or friction to the bolt; needs a check that it cannot jam the bolt or the jettison |
| Mechanical baulk / stop that keeps the handle from reaching "locked" unless engaged | Receiver area | Europa SM15833 (S11) | **Yes**: it changes the latch function. Out of our scope; cite as regulator precedent only |
| Mechanical interlock between canopies | Linkage | ASK 21 (S12) | **Yes**: out of scope |

### 5.3 What this means for the Extra NG choice
- The Extra NG is architecture **A1** (3 shoot bolts, one tube, 2 sliders) on a **side-hinged, jettisonable** canopy.
  Facts from R0/R1, not re-read here.
- Its documented partial-lock state is **F2 on all bolts at once** (handle not fully aft; wedge on roller). A single
  linkage full-travel sensor would catch that state.
- But a linkage sensor alone would miss **F1** (one bolt outside its lug because of frame distortion or a closed-but-not-
  seated canopy) and the vibration case **F5**. The T67 (S4), Europa (S11) and Duo Discus (S17) cases show that F1 and F5
  happen on real fleets.
- Proposed sensing for our concept: **3 receiver sensors (one per roller lug, fuselage side) + 1 linkage full-travel
  channel + 1 canopy-seated channel.** The receivers decide LOCKED; the linkage and seated channels give diagnosis and
  cross-checks. Mounting on the fuselage keeps jettison clean (no wires on the canopy).
- Mechanical indicator: a **colour band on each bolt** that is visible at the lug only at full engagement (roller on the
  flat), read from the rear seat for the rear bolt at least. Plus a placard of correct vs partial positions (M810 style).
  Both are paint and placard: no change to the latch.
- Check before adopting [Y]: whether the bolts are visible at all from the seats; whether a band can be seen past the
  roller; the space at each lug for a sensor; and that no sensor or bracket touches the carbon frame near the middle-pawl
  notch (R1 crack note).

### 5.4 Method for "any aircraft" (step list for the retrofit process)
1. **Map the latch.** Count the engaging elements. Note which side each one is on (canopy or fuselage), and which side its
   receiver is on. Note the hinge type and whether the canopy is attached when closed but unlocked.
2. **Classify** the architecture (A1–A7).
3. **List the look-alike states** for that type: try to reproduce F1–F4 on the ground (like the AAIB did on the T67, S5).
   Record each state with a photo.
4. **Choose sensing points** from table 5.1: at each receiver first; then the drive (linkage or handle) as a second
   channel; then canopy seated.
5. **Check for a legitimate second locked state** (vent, taxi gap). If one exists, the sensor must sit at the "closed"
   receiver (A2 rule).
6. **Choose mechanical indicators** from table 5.2 that do not alter the latch: colour band on the engaging element,
   placard of correct vs incorrect positions, reference mark at the handle.
7. **Set and verify** every sensor against a full-engagement gauge and a partial-engagement gauge; keep the record.
8. **Check flight loads**: sensor brackets and windows across the envelope (F5; Van's caution in R7).
9. **Write the procedure** with the alert: before take-off = stop and re-latch on the ground; in flight = fly, do not
   re-latch (F9).
