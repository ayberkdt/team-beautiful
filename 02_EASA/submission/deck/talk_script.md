### Slide 1 · Cover

We are Team Beetiful. We present LOCK-SENSE, a canopy-lock alert for the Extra NG.

### Slide 2 · A partly locked canopy looks the same as a locked one

*Speaker 1 · 32 s · 0:06–0:38*

The canopy of the Extra NG G-MIIL broke up at 184 knots. The AAIB found a bad frame bond. The BFU thought the canopy was not fully locked. The aircraft did not record the lock state, so nobody can settle this. The handles show the same 70 millimetre gap, locked or released. EASA AD 2025-0156 names a partly locked canopy as the main cause of openings. Our goal: fewer unnoticed unlocked take-offs, and a lock-state record.

### Slide 3 · Lock-related openings cluster in the initial climb

*Speaker 1 · 22 s · 0:38–1:00*

Our catalogue has 241 investigated canopy and door events. 126 were lock-related, and 16 were fatal. Most openings happen in the initial climb, on many aircraft types. On other types, every false 'locked' indication that we found came from one switch. It was on the handle, the drive or one pin.

### Slide 4 · Our choices reach a busy pilot and never take away power

*Speaker 2 · 31 s · 1:00–1:31*

For each element, we kept the option that reaches a busy pilot and never takes away engine power. We sense each bolt and the seat. One pin cannot see one bolt out. Inductive sensors give fewer false alerts. We use a voice without a lead tone, and a lap-belt pad. We removed engine shut-down, the gate and the throttle pawl. A fault there could take away power. So LOCK-SENSE only alerts.

### Slide 5 · Green needs all four sensors; a partly locked bolt gives red

*Speaker 2 · 25 s · 1:31–1:56*

Four inductive sensors sit on the fuselage side, so the jettison path does not change. Three watch the bolt tips. One watches the seated frame. A sensor sees steel only at full lock. Green needs all four sensors. An open or a short circuit gives 'not locked'. No single electrical fault gave a false green.

### Slide 6 · The red alert starts at line-up, before the take-off roll

*Speaker 3 · 32 s · 1:56–2:28*

The phase of flight sets the alerts. In taxi, an unlocked canopy gives only an amber lamp and one chime. At line-up, it gives a red lamp, the voice 'CANOPY, CANOPY' and the belt pad. With the lever trigger, the median pilot acts at 18 knots. An rpm trigger gives 31 knots. Pilots under high workload missed 38 percent of tones. Two or three cues act faster than one. The red lamp never waits.

### Slide 8 · 71× fewer unnoticed unsafe take-offs if pilots always act

## Likely jury questions (answers only from the report)

**Q1. Why not one microswitch at the pin, as in the task sheet?**

One pin cannot see one bolt out. In our model, one microswitch at one pin missed a single bolt out in 133 345 of 200 000 trials (0.67). The task-sheet set gives 4.6×. Our set gives 71.3× when the pilot always acts.

*Source: p. 5 Table 1; p. 6 §5, Table 3.*

**Q2. Why no take-off gate, throttle pawl or engine shut-down?**

These act on the engine or the throttle. A fault could take away power in flight. Figure 5 shows that a gate gives more on paper, but we removed it for safety. LOCK-SENSE only alerts.

*Source: p. 5 Table 1 (Barrier); p. 7 Figure 5.*

**Q3. What if pilots stop trusting the alert?**

Then the pilot sets the benefit. With distrust, the chosen set gives about 3× (2.8×). With the inductive sensor's own false-alert rate (0.14 per 1 000 flights), it gives 5.0×. In taxi, an unlocked canopy gives only an amber lamp and one chime. Step 1 measures the bolt travel ratio first. At 0.5 mm per mm, scatter gives about 51 false alerts per 1 000 flights.

*Source: p. 6 Table 3 note; p. 7 Table 4, §5; p. 4 Figure 3.*

**Q4. Would LOCK-SENSE have saved G-MIIL?**

Not against the AAIB cause. The system cannot detect a frame failure while the bolts stay locked. A one-off NDT of the frame bond stays the defence. But its log keeps the sensed lock state, so the next investigation has the record that G-MIIL did not have. It cannot prove that the frame was sound.

*Source: p. 3 §1; p. 7 §5, §6.*

**Q5. How do you know that a sensor fault cannot show 'locked'?**

Line monitoring turns an open or a short into 'not locked'. We ran thirteen fault cases. No single electrical fault gave a false green. Two combined cases did. One is a bolt out with its own sensor stuck ON. The other is all three sensors rigged early with one wrong gauge. The stuck sensor shows as a fault at the next powered opening. The step 9 gauge check and the handle check cover the rest. Next, we bench-test 30 sensors over 10 cycles.

*Source: p. 4 §3; p. 7 §5, §6.*
